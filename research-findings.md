# Case File: Bozeman-Area Junk Removal

**Target:** The small-operator junk removal landscape across Bozeman, Belgrade, Big Sky, and Three Forks, MT — competitor map, Facebook-positioning playbook, and national-service gap analysis for a pre-launch entrant (Chase's Junk Removal).

**One-line takeaway:** The valley has ~15 small haulers but only **one** with real brand gravity (Junk in the Trunk); the social field is nearly empty, pricing is opaque industry-wide, and **Big Sky has no resident operator** — the opening is a transparent-pricing, donation-first hauler that *owns* Big Sky and the construction wave, marketed through the exact Facebook community groups where these jobs are actually decided.

---

## The Audit

*Source-pool meta-analysis. This qualifies everything below — read it first.*

**The streets are half-lit.** Two layers, very different quality:

- **Operator existence & service menus: SATURATED and reliable.** Five sub-agents triangulated ~15 genuine local haulers from their own sites, Facebook, BBB, Nextdoor, Thumbtack, and chamber listings. The map of *who exists and what they claim to do* is `evidence: high`.

- **Review counts, ratings, pricing, and consumer sentiment: BLOCKED and thin.** This is the load-bearing caveat. **Yelp, Google Business, Angi, and HomeAdvisor all returned HTTP 403** to every agent — no live ratings were retrievable. **Reddit (r/Bozeman) and Nextdoor blocked automated reads** — zero verbatim customer complaints were captured. **The Meta Ad Library returned 403** — no paid-ad activity could be confirmed for anyone. So: every review count here is **self-reported by the business** or pulled from a stale search snippet, and every "gap" is inferred from *infrastructure and geography*, not from a single customer actually saying "I couldn't find anyone."

**What that means for you:** The competitive *structure* is trustworthy. The competitive *reputation data* is not — treat all review numbers as claims, not facts, and verify the demand gaps by phone and by reading the Bozeman Facebook groups yourself before you bet money on them. A chorus of self-reported five-star counts is not a finding.

**Pricing is a near-total blackout.** Almost no local operator publishes a dollar figure — the market runs on "free quote." Only the national broker **LoadUp** (~$79/single item) and two dumpster outfits publish rates. That opacity is itself one of your biggest openings (see Worth Pulling).

### 2. The Facebook & social landscape — your highest-leverage opening

`likelihood: likely` / `evidence: moderate` (FB follower figures are snippet-derived; Ad Library blocked).

- **One operator has meaningful social proof, and it's modest.** Junk in the Trunk: ~407 FB followers, the only one with a real recommendation footprint. **Every other operator is under ~120 followers; two are effectively dormant (≤20).** No competitor could be confirmed running Meta ads.
- **The discovery channel is uncontested.** Junk-removal jobs here get decided in community-group threads — "anyone know a good junk hauler?" posts in **The Real Ask Bozeman**, **Ask Bozeman**, **Bozeman Buy/Sell/Trade**, and on **Nextdoor** (~53,500 Bozeman-area neighbors). No operator works these systematically.
- **Mechanics that rank you locally (2025-current):** FB Recommendations volume + recency; group posts get ~10× the organic reach of Page posts; Reels out-reach static posts 2-3×; the "Very Responsive" badge (90% replies <15 min) is free and earnable in week one; external links get throttled ~70-80% (use "DM for a quote"); and **Google Business review *velocity*** is now a primary local signal — recent reviews beat a big stale pile.

### 3. The national service menu & pricing models (the benchmark)

`evidence: high` (triangulated across chains, brokers, and operator-education sources, dated 2025-2026).

- **Full service taxonomy** runs from core haul-away through estate/hoarding/foreclosure cleanouts, construction & demolition debris, light demolition, garage/attic/basement, yard & storm debris, dumpster rental, e-waste, mattress & appliance/freon recycling, donation routing, commercial/property-management contracts, hot-tub removal, and junk-plus-move hybrids.
- **Dominant pricing model = volume (fraction-of-truck):** ~$100-200 (1/8) → ~$550-800 (full), with rural markets running 10-25% *below* national. Per-item (LoadUp's model, ~$79+ first item), hourly ($75-150/two-person crew), and flat minimums ($75-150) round it out. Standard surcharges: stairs/long-carry, heavy debris (concrete/tile per-ton), freon ($25-75/unit), e-waste, mattress, same-day.
- **National trend that's now table-stakes:** donation-first / landfill-diversion positioning (chains claiming 60-70% diversion) and **upfront online pricing**. The locals here have adopted *neither* at any visible level — that's your wedge.

### 4. The Montana operating layer (what makes services viable here)

`evidence: high` for disposal facts (pulled directly from gallatinsolidwaste.org); `evidence: high` for growth/construction demand (multiple 2025-2026 sources).

- **Disposal reality:** Logan Landfill (Manhattan, ~25 mi from Bozeman) — MSW $27/ton, light C&D $48/ton, heavy C&D $58/ton, **refrigerant appliance evac $32/unit**, tires $58/ton. Bozeman Convenience Site (in-town) is faster **but bans refrigerant appliances, full trailer/dump-truck loads, and concrete** — and is closed Sun **and** Mon, shutting at 3:30pm. **A single fridge forces a 50-min round trip to Logan.**
- **Diversion partners:** Habitat ReStore (Belgrade), Pacific Steel (pays cash for scrap metal), Four Corners Recycling.
- **Demand is structurally strong and growing:** Bozeman ~60k and +1.8%/yr; **Belgrade is the fastest-growing city in MT** with a development pipeline in the thousands of units (Middletown alone ~4,686); ~497 new-construction homes listed in 2025; **Big Sky in a $150M+ resort build-out**; 74 homes sold >$2M in 2025 (vs. 11 in 2019).
- **Seasonality is a two-engine system:** valley peaks spring-summer (moves, remodels, cleanups); **Big Sky runs counter-seasonal** (winter resort season + year-round construction). Winter compresses Logan's hours and makes rural/ranch access hard.

## What Doesn't Add Up

*Contested claims with a light hypothesis matrix. These are the spots where the evidence wobbles.*

**A. Junk in the Trunk's "180-200+ five-star reviews" vs. a BBB establishment date of Oct 2024.**
Going from zero to ~200 reviews and BBB accreditation in well under a year is fast for this market.
- *H1 — Rebrand of an older entity:* an existing operation re-formed under a new LLC, carrying its reputation. (Most likely — fits the family-business profile.)
- *H2 — Aggressive, systematic review-gathering:* genuinely new but disciplined about asking. (Plausible — and if so, it's *imitable*; it's their playbook, not a moat.)
- *H3 — Inflated/self-reported number:* the count is a website claim; Google was unverifiable (403). (Can't rule out.)
- **Verdict:** `likelihood: roughly_even` between H1 and H2 / `evidence: low`. Either way, the takeaway holds: their lead is review-driven, recent, and **catchable** — not a structural moat. **Verify their actual Google count manually before treating it as the bar.**

**B. "No junk hauler is based in Big Sky" — proving a negative.**
The Big Sky agent ran 50+ searches and found only EcoSmash (compaction; website 404, likely defunct) and Big Sky Bin Service (valet trash) physically there.
- *H1 — Genuinely no resident hauler:* the economics (1-hr haul, seasonal) deter local basing. (Most likely.)
- *H2 — A resident operator exists but is invisible online* (word-of-mouth only). (Possible in a small resort town.)
- **Verdict:** `likelihood: likely` / `evidence: moderate`. Strong enough to *plan around*, not strong enough to *bet the company on* — confirm by asking Big Sky property managers directly.

**C. Out-of-state phone numbers on "local" operators.** Bozeman Junk Removal & Hauling LLC (WA 360), Treasure State Movers (Chicago 630), Common Waste (FL 954 secondary). Could be relocated owners, VoIP, or national parents in local clothing. `evidence: low` — flagged, not resolved.

**D. Quick Clear Junk Bozeman — operator or spam?** One agent flagged the site as lead-gen with embedded gambling links; another logged it as a real operator. **Treat as suspect** until verified.

## Worth Pulling

*The small details that could crack the case open.*

- **🔑 Big Sky is the unguarded flank.** No resident hauler, no one targeting resort/HOA/property-management/construction-turnover work, and — critically — **not one operator advertises a Big Sky premium despite the hour-long drive.** The whole market is mispricing the most affluent, highest-willingness-to-pay corner of your service area. A Big Sky-anchored brand (even valley-based but *Big-Sky-first* in marketing and pricing) faces almost no direct competition. Caveat: EcoSmash's apparent death is a warning that a pure Big Sky play has thin year-round volume — pair it with valley work.
- **🔑 Pricing transparency is wide open.** The entire local field hides behind "free quote." A published per-item + volume price list (the LoadUp move, localized) would be a genuine differentiator in a market where every competitor makes you call to find out anything.
- **🔑 Donation-first is unclaimed.** National table-stakes; *nobody local owns it.* In environmentally-minded Bozeman, "we donate/recycle first, landfill last — here's your receipt" (via Habitat ReStore + Pacific Steel) is both a margin play (lower tipping fees) and a marketing identity no competitor has taken.
- **Limitless is the one to actually watch.** Skid steer + mini excavator + dump trailer means they can do land clearing, grading, and real demolition — jobs pure haulers can't touch. They're your most *capable* competitor even if JITT is the most *visible*.
- **The recurring-revenue proof points exist.** Common Waste runs subscription pickup plans; Big Sky Bin Service runs $35/mo valet trash. Recurring property-management/HOA contracts are being validated at the edges — nobody has consolidated them.
- **The refrigerant-appliance trip is a productizable pain.** Because a single fridge forces a Logan run, a flat-rate "appliance & fridge pickup" that batches units and amortizes the trip is a clean, ownable offering.

## Loose Threads

*Open questions and recommended follow-ups before you commit capital.*

1. **Verify the real reputation bar.** Manually open Google Business + Yelp for Junk in the Trunk, Limitless, and 406 R&H and record *actual* review counts/ratings. The 403 wall means we never confirmed them.
2. **Read the demand in the wild.** Spend an evening in The Real Ask Bozeman, Ask Bozeman, and r/Bozeman searching "junk," "haul," "cleanout." Capture verbatim complaints and unmet asks — that's the consumer voice this run couldn't reach.
3. **Phone-check Big Sky.** Call 3-5 Big Sky property managers / HOAs: who do they use for cleanouts and construction debris, and are they happy? Confirms or kills the #1 gap.
4. **Re-confirm tipping fees by phone** (Logan 406-582-2492) before building any price model — the fee pages carry no "last updated" date.
5. **Pin down equipment strategy.** Your pricing power on C&D and Big Sky jobs depends on whether you match Limitless's heavy-equipment capability or stay a clean hauler. Decide early.
